01  The Week in Brief AI · Geopolitics · Tech · Funding

Five stories from the intersection of AI, geopolitics and the technology economy — curated and commented.

This week, the collision between AI geopolitics and commercial ambition reached a new intensity. OpenAI proposed handing the US government a 5% equity stake, Anthropic moved to block Chinese engineers from accessing Claude, and Wayve's $8.5bn employee tender confirmed that London's autonomous vehicle sector remains a serious global contender. Beneath the deal-making, a harder question is taking shape: who controls AI infrastructure, and on whose terms?

OpenAI proposes handing Trump administration 5% stake in the company

— Financial Times

Sam Altman has proposed donating 5% of OpenAI's equity to a US sovereign wealth fund, framing it as a way to give the American public a share in the financial gains from the AI boom. The talks are early-stage but signal a deliberate effort by Altman to embed OpenAI within the political architecture of the Trump administration.

Why it matters

If completed, this would be the first time a leading AI lab formally ceded partial ownership to a government, blurring the line between private tech and state interest in ways that will reverberate across every democracy watching from the sidelines.

The London lens

The UK's own AI strategy review, led by Andy Burnham's team, is explicitly trying to resist the model of AI development shaped entirely by US companies — this deal would make that task considerably harder.

Anthropic moves to close loopholes that allow Chinese access to Claude

— Financial Times

Anthropic has discovered that Chinese engineers are circumventing export restrictions to access Claude, and is now working to close the technical pathways that make this possible. The company's own engineers are still finding new routes through its restrictions, underlining how difficult enforcement is in practice.

Why it matters

This is the frontier of AI export control: not sanctions on hardware but a real-time cat-and-mouse game over access to model weights and APIs, and neither side has yet found a definitive answer.

Tech context

AI models can often be accessed through indirect routes — third-party API wrappers, resellers operating in neutral jurisdictions, or cloud providers who do not enforce end-user checks — making technical restrictions far harder to enforce than traditional trade controls on physical goods.

Wayve launches $85M employee tender offer at $8.5bn valuation

— TechCrunch

London-based autonomous vehicle startup Wayve has opened an $85 million employee tender offer, allowing staff to sell existing shares at a valuation of $8.5 billion. The move reflects a broader trend of AI startups using secondary liquidity as a talent retention tool in lieu of an imminent IPO.

Why it matters

Wayve's valuation makes it one of the most valuable private AI companies in Europe, and the tender structure signals that its backers are prepared to hold for the long term rather than rushing to public markets.

The London lens

Wayve remains the clearest evidence that London can produce globally competitive deep-tech AI companies at scale — the $8.5bn valuation places it well above most European peers and directly in competition with US and Chinese autonomous driving programmes.

Burnham's team looks to revamp UK's AI strategy

— Financial Times

Andy Burnham's team is working on a revised UK AI strategy that would shift the focus away from attracting US hyperscalers and towards making AI work for local communities and public services. The review reflects growing unease within government about the current approach, which critics argue has benefited American companies more than British citizens.

Why it matters

A reorientation of UK AI policy towards public benefit rather than inward investment would represent a significant break from the post-Sunak consensus and could define the country's regulatory posture for the next decade.

The London lens

For UK AI companies and founders, this review will determine the procurement, regulatory, and funding environment they operate in — the direction it takes matters as much as any single funding round.

Anthropic discussing custom AI chip with Samsung

— TechCrunch

Anthropic is in discussions with Samsung to develop a custom AI chip, following OpenAI's announcement of its own silicon partnership with Broadcom just a week earlier. The move signals that frontier AI labs are accelerating their push to reduce dependence on Nvidia by building proprietary hardware tailored to their specific model architectures.

Why it matters

If the leading AI labs successfully develop their own chips, the current Nvidia-dominated supply chain for AI compute could fragment significantly within three to five years, reshaping the economics of who can afford to train frontier models.

Tech context

Custom chips, often called ASICs, can be designed to run a specific company's model architecture far more efficiently than general-purpose GPUs — the trade-off is a years-long development cycle and the need to manufacture at scale to justify the cost.

Watch This

Battery startups serving AI data centres are reporting 'crazy' demand for energy storage systems that smooth out power surges from GPU clusters — if this supply chain cannot scale fast enough, it becomes a physical constraint on how quickly new data centre capacity can actually be brought online.

02  Minds Worth Following Essays · Long reads · Podcasts

The sharpest thing written, said or published this week — on AI, ideas, or anything worth thinking about.

Financial Times

Altman's AI safety proposal: let us win, or everybody loses

This piece dissects Sam Altman's framing of AI safety as a geopolitical argument: that only a US-led, American-company-dominated world order can keep AI safe, and that any restriction on OpenAI's growth therefore endangers humanity. The author argues this is a self-serving construction that conflates the interests of an American oligopoly with the interests of the world. It is one of the sharper readings of how safety rhetoric is being used as a competitive moat.

MIT Technology Review

LLMs are stuck in a groupthink groove. This startup is trying to get them out.

Ask any major language model for a random number between one and ten and you will almost always get seven — a simple demonstration of a deeper problem: LLMs trained on the same internet data with similar techniques converge on the same outputs and reasoning patterns. A startup is attempting to solve this by introducing structured diversity into the model ensemble, so that different models disagree with each other before a final answer is synthesised. The practical implication is significant: for any organisation using AI to make decisions, homogeneous model outputs create a false sense of confidence in conclusions that are really just the average of the same training data.

Financial Times

AI is helping workers sue their bosses. It may break the system.

AI tools are dramatically lowering the cost and complexity of bringing employment tribunal claims, and the UK tribunal system is already struggling to cope with the resulting surge in caseload. The piece explores a genuine tension: access to justice is improving for individual workers, but the infrastructure built to deliver that justice has not scaled with demand. For senior managers and HR leaders, the practical upshot is that the risk profile of employment disputes has shifted materially, and the assumption that most claims will settle rather than proceed is becoming less reliable.

03  Staying Human Body · Mind · The Analogue World

Your health, your attention, your life off the screen. Evidence-led and never preachy.

The Practice

The case for a deliberate no-screen gap after waking

A growing body of research on cortisol and attention suggests that the first 30 to 60 minutes after waking represent a neurologically distinct window in which the brain is transitioning from a diffuse, low-arousal state to focused wakefulness. Reaching for a phone during this period — and the email, news, and social feeds it delivers — compresses that transition and primes the threat-detection and reactive-response systems before the prefrontal cortex is fully online. The practical result is a cognitive register that is reactive rather than deliberate for the hours that follow. Andrew Huberman's protocol, drawn from his work on the neuroscience of stress and focus, recommends using this window for natural light exposure, light movement, or unstructured thought rather than information consumption — not as a productivity hack, but as a structural condition for the kind of slow, strategic thinking that senior roles actually require.

The Edit
City on the Edge — can San Francisco's soul survive techno-capitalism?

Jonathan Weber's book-length argument is that San Francisco's current condition — cleaned up, surveilled, optimised — is not a recovery from its years of dysfunction but a different kind of ruin: the replacement of a genuinely pluralist city with a company town that has laundered its aesthetics. Read it as a provocation about what London might become if it continues to position itself as the European answer to Silicon Valley.

There will be no more games like Minecraft without maths

The central argument here is precise and under-discussed: the cuts to pure mathematics and computational science in UK universities are not abstract threats to academic culture but a direct attack on the talent pipeline that produces the engineers behind generational software products. Minecraft, the piece notes, was built on procedural generation algorithms — the kind of thinking that only comes from rigorous mathematical training, not from vocational coding courses.

How AI could make markets worse

Using the framework of information economics, this piece argues that AI does not simply make markets more efficient — it can concentrate informational advantage in ways that worsen the underlying problem of asymmetric information that markets are supposed to resolve. The Spotify story this week, in which a song was apparently streamed to number one to profit a prediction market bet, is a live illustration of exactly this dynamic.
